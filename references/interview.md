# Personalization interview

Interview conversationally, one topic at a time, with at most three short questions per turn. Reuse answers already given. Offer a quick setup and optional deeper style calibration. Explain why a question matters. A skipped answer remains unknown; do not fill it from demographics or guesses.

## 1. Purpose and scope

- What should I call you, and what work or interests do you want help with?
- What do you find yourself explaining repeatedly to agents?
- Which topics belong in shared personal memory? Which projects need a separate vault or access boundary?
- Should I save durable facts and preferences as we work, or only when you explicitly ask?

Store approved facts in `Personal/Profile.md`; scope and capture mode in `rules.md`. Use optional People notes only when the user wants that context saved. Do not request legal names, birthdays, family details or health information just to populate the template.

## 2. Communication and working relationship

- Do you prefer a short answer first, a full explanation, or a recommendation followed by evidence?
- How direct should disagreement be? When should I challenge an assumption?
- How much initiative should I take, and which actions need your approval?
- Do you prefer a recommendation, a comparison of options, or a question when something is uncertain?
- What makes an assistant frustrating or helpful for you?

Save explicit choices in `Personal/Preferences.md`. Describe behavior, not a personality diagnosis. Warmth, humor, concision and vocabulary can adapt by context. Preserve honesty, uncertainty and independent judgment.

## 3. Documents and deliverables

- Who usually reads your documents and what should they be able to do afterward?
- Preferred length, structure and opening: executive summary, argument, story, or decision request?
- Preferences for headings, bullets, prose, tables, charts, citations, footnotes and acronym definitions?
- Preferred file formats, editable originals, page size, typography, language and locale?
- Should business, technical, learning and public-writing documents use different defaults?
- Show or describe an example you like and one you dislike. What specifically works or fails?

Keep context-specific rules separate. “Short chat replies” does not imply “short research papers.” Evidence is either direct user preference, observed pattern awaiting confirmation, or an approved example with source/date.

## 4. Voice calibration (optional)

Ask for selected writing samples already accessible to the agent; do not require uploads. Establish which parts the person authored, edited or quoted. Extract a few observable features: sentence rhythm, paragraph development, vocabulary, argument structure, humor and common dislikes. Cite examples privately. Draft a short neutral sample, request corrections, then record accepted rules in `Personal/Voice.md`.

Without samples, use explicit preferences and mark voice calibration pending. Do not call this model training. Do not copy sample facts, personal experiences or third-party phrases into new public prose.

## 5. Privacy and retention

- What must never be saved? Are work and personal accounts allowed to share this vault?
- May daily review read full visible conversation text, or only selected session summaries?
- Which accounts, projects and session folders are in scope? Exclude sensitive workplaces and other people's accounts unless explicitly authorized.
- How should forgotten or superseded information be handled, including source copies and backups?

Never request credentials. Explain that local transcripts may contain sensitive details; broad history import is optional and requires an explicit scope. The script is not a comprehensive personal-data or secret detector.

## 6. Installation and daily review

- Operating system, existing Obsidian vault and selected OneDrive account/folder?
- Which harnesses and devices should read this memory? Which startup files may be edited?
- Timezone and daily review time? Prefer after the day ends, for example 02:00 for the previous calendar day, with catch-up after downtime.
- Which runtime will execute the review, and does it have persistent access to the sources and vault?
- Should successful runs stay quiet or report changes? How should failures be surfaced?

Create a compact setup summary: exact paths, personal choices, capture scope, intended schedule, approval boundaries and unresolved answers. Ask only for missing authority or material choices. Then apply what the user has authorized. Record all setup claims as user-reported or proposed, with interview source and date.
